The history of Talkomatic:

  • 1973 - The First Chat Room

    Created by Doug Brown and David Woolley, two students working on the PLATO system at the University of Illinois. Doug built the first prototype; David extended it with multiple channels and the ability to make a conversation private.

  • 1970s - The PLATO Era

    PLATO (Programmed Logic for Automatic Teaching Operations) was a computer-based education system that grew into the first real online community. Alongside Talkomatic it carried message boards, email, instant messaging and multiplayer games, most of them years ahead of their better-known successors.

  • 2013-2014 - Rebuilt for the Web

    Work on a web version began in September 2013, and the first browser-to-browser chat took place on 11 March 2014. The WebSocket standard finally made it possible to send every keystroke live without any special software.

  • 2014-2015 - Private Rooms and Accounts

    Version 2.0 added private rooms in May 2014. Version 3.0 followed in July 2015 with social login, making it quicker to get in.

  • 2018 - Kept Alive

    Steve Zoppi re-hosted and updated the service to version 3.2, and with Ray Ozzie's support it ran across several domains.

  • March 2024 - The Original Goes Offline

    After a decade on the web, the original service shut down.

  • Talkomatic Classic

    Talkomatic returned at talkomatic.co as a free, open-source project so that the first chat room ever made would not simply disappear. It is run by its community rather than owned by a company, and the code is public for anyone to read, host or improve.
